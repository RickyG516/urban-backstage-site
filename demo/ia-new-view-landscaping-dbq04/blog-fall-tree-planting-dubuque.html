Why Fall Is the Best Time to Plant Trees in Dubuque

August 22, 2026 · New View Landscaping

Spring gets all the attention as planting season, but for trees and shrubs specifically, fall is usually the better call in our climate, and the window for it is narrower than most people realize.

The reason comes down to what a new tree actually needs to survive: time to grow roots before it has to deal with stress. Plant in spring and a young tree has to establish roots while also pushing out new leaves and dealing with summer heat, all at once. Plant in fall and the tree can put its energy into root growth through the cooler weeks before winter dormancy, with no leaf or heat demands competing for that energy. By the time next summer's heat hits, it's got a head start most spring-planted trees don't have.

In Dubuque, that fall window runs roughly from early September through mid-October, before the ground gets cold enough to stall root growth. Planting too late into November is a real risk, the tree doesn't get enough time to establish before winter locks the ground up.

Zone matters here too. Dubuque sits in USDA hardiness zone 5a to 5b, which is colder than a lot of tree stock sold at big box stores is actually rated for. A tree that looked great on the lot in a warmer-zone nursery isn't necessarily going to make it through a real Iowa winter. We plant species proven for this zone specifically, not whatever's discounted that week.

Whatever the season, new plantings need consistent watering through their first year, that's the single biggest factor in whether a tree or shrub actually survives long-term, more than the planting date itself. Fall plantings still need watering right up until the ground freezes, people sometimes assume cooler weather means they can stop, and that's when newly planted trees get lost over winter.

If you've been putting off adding trees or shrubs because you missed the spring window, you didn't miss anything, you're actually heading into the better time to do it.
