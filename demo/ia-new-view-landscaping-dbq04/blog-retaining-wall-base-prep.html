Retaining Wall Basics: Why Base Prep Matters More Than the Blocks

July 22, 2026 · New View Landscaping

Most people shopping for a retaining wall spend their time thinking about the block, color, texture, natural stone versus manufactured. That stuff matters for how it looks. It has almost nothing to do with whether the wall is still standing straight in ten years.

What actually determines whether a wall holds is what happens before the first block goes down. Excavation depth, base compaction, and drainage behind the wall. Skip or shortcut any of those three and it doesn't matter how good the block looks on day one, you're looking at a wall that leans, bulges, or fails within a handful of years.

Base prep means digging down to solid, undisturbed soil, not just scraping off the grass, and compacting a gravel base in layers so it doesn't settle unevenly later. A wall built on a shallow, uncompacted base is basically sitting on soil that's still going to move. When it moves, the wall moves with it.

Drainage is the part almost nobody thinks about until it's too late. Soil holds water, and water behind a retaining wall creates pressure the wall isn't built to handle. That's why a wall built right has gravel backfill and drainage behind it, so water has somewhere to go instead of pushing on the wall from behind every time it rains or the snow melts.

This is also where the height and slope of the wall change the math. A two-foot garden wall and a six-foot wall holding back a driveway are not the same engineering problem, and taller walls sometimes need reinforcement (geogrid) tied back into the soil behind them to stay put long-term.

If you're getting quotes on a retaining wall, ask what's happening below grade before you ask about the block color. That's the part that decides whether you're doing this again in five years.
