Why Your Yard Won't Stop Pooling Water

July 8, 2026 · New View Landscaping

If there's a spot in your yard that turns into a puddle every time it rains, the instinct is usually to blame the grass. Re-seed it, add sod, maybe some topsoil. That almost never fixes it, because the problem usually isn't the grass. It's what's underneath it.

Water moves downhill. If a low spot in your yard has nowhere to drain to, it's going to sit there no matter what's growing on top of it. This is a grading problem, not a lawn problem, and it's the most common issue we get called out for that homeowners have already tried to fix themselves at least once.

The usual causes: a yard that was never properly graded away from the house when it was built, soil that's settled unevenly over the years, or a downspout dumping water straight into a low area instead of routing it somewhere useful. Sometimes it's all three at once.

The fix starts with reading the actual slope of the property, not guessing at it. We look at where water is coming from, where it wants to go, and where it's getting stuck. In a lot of cases that means regrading the low spot so it drains toward the street or a natural runoff point. In others, it means a French drain to carry water underground to somewhere it can actually go.

One thing worth knowing before you call anyone: if water is pooling within a few feet of your foundation, that's not just an annoyance, that's a long-term risk to the foundation itself. That's the kind of drainage issue we treat as a priority, not a someday project.

If you've got a spot that never dries out, it's worth having someone actually look at the grade before you spend more money on sod that's just going to sit in the same puddle next spring.
