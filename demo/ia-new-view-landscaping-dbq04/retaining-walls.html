Retaining Walls

Walls that hold — built with proper base and drainage, not just stacked block.

A retaining wall is only as good as what's underneath it. New View excavates and compacts a real base on every wall, adds drainage behind the block, and steps footings the way a slope actually demands.

Built for erosion control, grade changes, and raised beds — block or natural stone, sized to the job.

Dubuque winters are hard on anything built into the ground. Freeze-thaw cycles push against a wall that doesn't have proper drainage behind it, which is exactly how a wall built on the cheap starts to lean or bow within a few seasons. Gravel backfill and a compacted base aren't optional extras here, they're the difference between a wall that's still straight in ten years and one you're rebuilding in three.

Taller walls (generally over about four feet, depending on the slope and soil) sometimes need geogrid reinforcement tied back into the hillside behind them, and very tall or load-bearing walls can require engineering review and a permit through the city. We handle that conversation as part of the quote, not as a surprise after the wall's already planned.

  • ✓ Drainage behind the wall, not just in front of it
  • ✓ Block or natural stone

Common Questions

Retaining Walls FAQs

Do I need a permit for a retaining wall in Dubuque?

Depends on height and location, shorter walls typically don't, taller or load-bearing walls usually do. We'll tell you straight during the estimate rather than let it become a surprise.

How long does a properly built retaining wall last?

Decades, if the base and drainage are done right. Most wall failures we get called out to fix trace back to a shallow base or no drainage behind the block, not the block itself.

Block or natural stone, which is better?

Both hold up fine when built correctly. It's mostly a look and budget decision, natural stone runs higher but has a different character than manufactured block.
