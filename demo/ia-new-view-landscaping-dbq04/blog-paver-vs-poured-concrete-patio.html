Paver vs. Poured Concrete for a Dubuque Patio

August 15, 2026 · New View Landscaping

This is the question we get asked most on patio projects, and the honest answer is both can work, but they fail differently, and that matters more here than it does somewhere that doesn't get a real winter.

Poured concrete is one solid slab. When it's done right it looks clean and uniform. The problem in our climate is freeze-thaw. Water gets into hairline cracks, freezes, expands, and over enough winters that turns into real cracking. Once a concrete slab cracks, the whole slab shows it, and fixing it usually means replacing sections, not spot repairs.

Pavers are individual units set on a compacted base with sand between the joints. That joint spacing is actually the advantage in freeze-thaw conditions, the whole surface can flex slightly with ground movement instead of cracking as one rigid piece. If a single paver does get damaged, you pull that one and replace it. You're not redoing the whole patio.

Pavers do cost more upfront than a basic poured slab. Where that evens out is long-term maintenance and the fact that a properly base-prepped paver patio tends to hold up better over a lot of Iowa winters than concrete does. It's not that concrete always fails, a well-poured, properly reinforced slab can last a long time too. It's that pavers have more built-in tolerance for the freeze-thaw cycle this area deals with every year.

The base underneath matters more than the surface material either way. A paver patio on a shallow or poorly compacted base will heave just like bad concrete will crack. That prep work is where we spend most of our time on any patio project, regardless of what's going on top.

If you're deciding between the two, think about how the patio connects to the rest of the yard too, pavers are easier to match to a walkway or match against a retaining wall built with similar stone. That's usually the tiebreaker once the durability question is settled.
