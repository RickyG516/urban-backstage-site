Permanent lights are a bigger decision than a box of strands from the store. They go on your house and stay there, and you will live with the result every night of the year. A good estimate should leave you with clear answers, not a pile of new questions. Here is a checklist you can bring to any conversation about permanent lights in Dubuque, including ours.

## Start with the layout

Ask where the lights will run and why. A roofline is the usual starting point, but many homes also have peaks, dormers, a garage line, or a porch that deserve a look. A good installer walks the house with you and talks about what will look right from the street, not just what is easiest to install.

Questions worth asking:

- Which parts of the house will be lit, and which will be left out?
- How will corners, peaks and gaps in the roofline be handled?
- Can the layout grow later if you want to add the garage or a second section?

If you are still comparing this with hanging lights each year, our post on [permanent lights versus seasonal lights in Dubuque](/blog/permanent-lights-vs-seasonal-dubuque/) lays out what you repeat with the seasonal approach.

## Ask about controls

Controls decide how much you enjoy the system after the install day. Find out how you turn the lights on and off, how you change colors, and how you set a schedule.

- Is control handled from a phone, a remote, a wall switch, or a mix?
- What happens if your home internet goes down?
- Can you set different looks for different nights without calling anyone?

Ask for a walk through of the controls before the job is finished, so you are not learning them alone later.

## Power and wiring

Lights need power, and where that power comes from matters. Ask how the system connects to your home, where the power supply will sit, and whether anything needs to be added or changed to your electrical setup. You want a clear answer on how it is done safely and tidily, with wiring that is not left hanging or exposed.

## What the lights are made of

You do not need to become an expert in materials, but you should know a few basics. Ask what the lights are rated for outdoors, how they handle cold and snow, and how they are attached to the house so they hold up through wind and ice. Winters here are hard on anything left outside, so this is worth a direct question.

## Warranty and service

Before you sign, find out what is covered and who handles problems. Ask for the details in writing rather than relying on a general statement.

- What is covered, and for how long?
- If a section stops working, who comes out and how do you reach them?
- Is there a fee for service visits, or is it included?
- What happens if you sell the house?

A company that is easy to reach after the install is worth more than one that is only easy to reach before it.

## The roof and the house

Ask how the installer protects your home during the work. That includes how they attach the lights, what they do around shingles and trim, and how they leave the house when the job is done. You should feel comfortable that the work is careful and that anything touched is left in good shape.

## Timing

Calendars fill up as the holidays approach, so ask when the earliest and latest realistic dates are. We cover the planning side in our post on [when to book holiday lighting in Iowa](/blog/when-to-book-holiday-lighting-iowa/). The short version is that earlier conversations give you more choice.

## Red flags

A few things should slow you down:

- Vague answers about controls, power, or service.
- No site visit before a final plan is given.
- Pressure to decide on the spot.
- Nothing in writing.

## FAQ

**How do I choose between permanent lights companies?**
Compare how clearly each one answers questions about layout, controls, power, warranty and service. A site visit and written details are good signs.

**Can permanent lights be controlled from my phone?**
Many systems offer phone control, but the details vary. Ask exactly how control works and what happens if your internet is down.

**Should I ask about service after the install?**
Yes. Find out who handles problems, how to reach them, and what is covered, and get it in writing before you sign.
