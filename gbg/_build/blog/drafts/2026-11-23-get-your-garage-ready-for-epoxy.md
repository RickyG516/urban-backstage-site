A new garage floor starts well before anyone opens a can. The work you do ahead of time, and the questions you ask about the slab, shape how the floor looks and lasts. If you are planning an epoxy floor in Dubuque, this guide covers what to clear out, what to ask, and what the day itself usually looks like.

## Decide if epoxy is the right fit

If you are still deciding between a coating system and a thin paint, our post on [epoxy garage floors versus garage floor paint](/blog/epoxy-garage-floor-vs-paint/) compares the two. Once you know you want a coating, the rest of this list will help you get ready.

## Clear the garage

The floor needs to be completely empty and accessible. That means moving cars, tools, shelves, bikes, bins, and anything else that sits on the concrete. Plan somewhere to put it all, since a garage holds more than most people expect.

A few tips:

- Start a week or two ahead so it does not become a last minute scramble.
- Move small items first, then the heavy ones.
- Check the walls and floor edges for things stored low, like paint cans or boxes.
- Decide what you actually want to keep. A coating job is a good excuse to let go of some clutter.

Where the stuff goes matters too. A driveway or a spare room works if the weather cooperates, and you will want a plan for rain or snow.

## Look at the slab yourself

Walk the floor and note what you see. You are not expected to diagnose anything, but your notes help the estimate.

- Cracks, chips, or pitted areas.
- Oil stains and spots where fluids have soaked in.
- Old paint or sealer that is peeling.
- Damp areas, white powder, or a musty smell, which can point to moisture.
- Places where water collects or runs under the garage door.

Take photos with your phone. They make it easier to describe the floor and compare it later.

## Ask about the concrete

A coating is only as good as the concrete under it, so these questions are worth asking:

- How will the surface be prepared, and does that include grinding?
- How are cracks and chips repaired before the coating goes on?
- How will moisture in the slab be checked?
- What happens if the slab turns out to have a problem on the day?

Clear answers here are a sign that the contractor cares about the part of the job you cannot see once it is finished.

## Think about temperature and timing

Coatings are sensitive to the temperature of the slab and the air, and Iowa weather does not always cooperate in the colder months. Ask what range the product needs and how the job is handled when the garage is cold. A heated garage or a mild stretch of weather can make a big difference. Our post on [using the fall window for driveway sealing](/blog/driveway-sealing-iowa-fall-window/) explains the same idea for outdoor surfaces.

## What to expect on the day

Every job is different, but a typical day follows a pattern:

- The crew checks the floor and walks the plan with you.
- The surface is cleaned and prepared, which can be loud and dusty.
- Cracks and chips are repaired.
- The coating goes down in steps, with time between them.

Plan to keep the garage clear and keep kids and pets out of the area. Ask ahead about noise, access to the house, and where you can park in the meantime.

## After the job

Ask when you can walk on the floor, when the car can go back in, and when it is safe to bring your belongings back. Follow what your contractor tells you, and do not rush it. A floor that is used too early can be marked or damaged, which defeats the point of the work.

Also ask how to care for it. Most coatings clean up with simple tools and mild products, but your contractor should tell you what is safe to use on yours.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to prepare a garage for epoxy?**
Plan a week or two to clear it out comfortably. The contractor will tell you how long the job itself takes once they see the slab.

**Do I need to fix cracks before epoxy?**
Cracks and chips should be repaired as part of the prep. Ask your contractor how they handle them before the coating is applied.

**Can I stay home during the job?**
Usually yes, but expect noise and dust during prep. Keep kids and pets away from the garage and ask what to expect for access.
