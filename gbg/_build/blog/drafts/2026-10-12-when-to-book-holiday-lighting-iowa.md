Most people think about holiday lights the week the decorations come out at the store. By then, anyone who installs lights for a living is already working through a list of homes that called weeks earlier. If you want lights up for the season in Dubuque, the best time to start the conversation is earlier than feels natural.

## Why the calendar fills up

Holiday lighting has a short window and a hard deadline. Nobody wants lights installed on December 28th. Everyone wants them glowing before Thanksgiving weekend, or at the latest before the first big family gathering of December. That squeezes most of the demand into a few weeks.

Iowa weather adds pressure. Crews work outside, on ladders and rooflines. Wind, ice, and snow can push a job back a day or more. A schedule that looks fine on paper in early November gets tight fast once a few days are lost to conditions. Booking early gives a company room to move your job without missing the date you care about.

There is also a practical limit. A crew can only complete so many homes in a day, and each home has its own roofline, height, and access. Larger homes take longer. The later you ask, the fewer open days are left.

## What to decide before the estimate

You do not need to have everything figured out. But a few decisions make the estimate faster and more accurate.

**Which parts of the house.** Do you want the front roofline only, or the garage, the dormers, the porch, and the back deck too? Walk the outside of your home and note the lines you actually see from the street and from your own windows.

**How much you want to handle yourself.** Some homeowners like putting up wreaths and trees and just want the roofline done. Others want everything handled. Knowing which camp you are in keeps the conversation simple.

**Seasonal or permanent.** This is the biggest fork. Seasonal installs go up, come down, and get stored. Permanent lights stay mounted year round, so there is no hanging or takedown each season. If you are leaning permanent, say so early, because it changes what the site walk needs to cover.

**Your timeline.** Pick a date you want lights on by. Be honest about it. If a family event or a neighborhood tradition sets the deadline, say so up front.


## What a site walk covers

A site walk is a short visit where someone looks at your home in person. It is not a hard sell, and you should not feel rushed. Here is what usually gets covered.

The first thing is the roofline itself. How long are the runs, how many corners and peaks are there, and what material are the fascia boards and soffits made of? Those details shape what mounting method makes sense.

Next is access. Is the ground level, or does the yard slope? Are there trees, shrubs, or a deck in the way of a ladder? Can power be reached from where the lights need to connect? A good walk looks for outlets and the best spot for a controller.

Then comes the layout conversation. You point out what you want lit and what you would rather skip. Many homes look better with fewer lit areas, not more, and an honest installer will tell you when a simpler plan makes sense.

Finally, you talk timing. This is where booking early pays off. If the schedule is open, you pick a window. If it is tight, you hear that plainly and can decide whether to wait, adjust the plan, or aim for next season.

You should leave the walk knowing what is planned, what the install involves, and when it is likely to happen. If any of that is fuzzy, ask.

## A simple way to plan ahead

In early fall, walk your house and decide what you want lit. Pick your target date. Then request an estimate while the weather is friendly and calendars still have room. If you wait until the first cold snap, you can still ask. You may just find fewer options. ## FAQ

**How early should I ask about holiday lighting?**
As early in the fall as you can. Calendars fill as the holidays get closer, and weather delays eat into the schedule. Asking early gives you more choices on dates.

**Do I need to be home for the site walk?**
It helps, since you can point out what you want lit and what you want left alone. If you cannot be there, you can share your wishes ahead of time and clear access to the outside of the house.

**Can I switch from seasonal to permanent lights later?**
Yes. Many homeowners try seasonal first and move to permanent after a few years of ladders and storage bins. A site walk can show you what a permanent system would look like on your roofline.
